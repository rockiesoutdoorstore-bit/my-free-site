Glossary

Clear, concise definitions of terms you will encounter frequently.

A–D

Animism — The view that the world is full of persons (human and other-than-human) with agency, rather than inert objects.

Blót — A ritual offering, especially in Heathen contexts; historically often involved animal sacrifice, today more commonly food, drink, or other gifts.

Coven — A small working group of witches, traditionally associated with Wicca but used more broadly.

Deity / God / Goddess — Terms for divine beings. Usage varies widely across polytheistic, soft-polytheist, and other frameworks.

E–M

Eclectic — Drawing from multiple sources rather than a single tradition.

Esbat — In many Wiccan and witchcraft contexts, a ritual held at the full (or sometimes new) moon.

Heathenry — Modern practice of Germanic/Norse pre-Christian religion.

Magic / Magick — Intentional work with subtle forces, symbols, or will; definitions and methods vary enormously.

N–S

Pagan / Paganism — Broad umbrella for modern (and sometimes ancient) polytheistic, animistic, or nature-centered spiritualities. Usage is contested and context-dependent.

Polytheism — Belief in or practice involving multiple gods. “Hard” polytheism treats deities as distinct individuals; “soft” polytheism may see them as aspects of a greater whole.

Reconstructionism — Approach that prioritizes historical and archaeological evidence in rebuilding ancient religious practice.

Sabbat — One of the seasonal festivals of the Wheel of the Year (in Wiccan and related contexts).

T–Z

Tradition — A specific lineage, set of practices, or cultural stream within the broader pagan landscape.

Wheel of the Year — The modern eight-festival seasonal calendar (solstices, equinoxes, and cross-quarter days) observed by many pagans and witches.

Wicca — A modern pagan religion originating in the mid-20th century, emphasizing ritual, seasonal celebration, and often a Goddess and God.

This glossary is introductory. Many terms have longer histories and contested meanings. Always check how a particular community or author is using a word.
