Green Witchcraft

A nature-centered approach that prioritizes relationship with plants, the land, seasonal cycles, and practical herbal and ecological knowledge.

Core Orientation

Green witchcraft (sometimes called green magic or earth-based witchcraft) places the living world at the center of practice. Rather than focusing primarily on formal ritual systems or specific pantheons, it emphasizes observation, reciprocity, and working with the materials and rhythms of the place where you live.

Practitioners often develop deep knowledge of local plants, weather patterns, soil, and wildlife. Magic tends to be practical — healing, protection, abundance, and alignment with natural cycles — and is frequently expressed through gardening, foraging, herbalism, and kitchen or home-based work.

Common Practices

  • Working with herbs, flowers, trees, and other plant allies
  • Observing and celebrating seasonal changes
  • Creating outdoor or garden altars and sacred spaces
  • Herbal remedies, teas, oils, and simple charms
  • Ecological awareness and land stewardship as spiritual practice

Relationship to Other Paths

Green witchcraft can stand alone or blend with Wicca, Druidry, folk magic, or eclectic practice. It is less concerned with historical reconstruction and more with direct, present-day relationship with the more-than-human world.
