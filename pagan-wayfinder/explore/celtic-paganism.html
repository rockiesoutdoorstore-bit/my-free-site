Celtic Paganism

Modern spiritual paths inspired by the mythologies, languages, and cultural traditions of the Celtic-speaking peoples of Ireland, Scotland, Wales, Brittany, and beyond.

Scope and Diversity

“Celtic Paganism” is a broad umbrella. It can include reconstructionist approaches focused on specific cultures (Irish, Welsh, Gaulish, etc.), as well as more eclectic or Druidic practices that draw heavily on Celtic myth and symbolism. The historical Celtic-speaking world was itself diverse across time and geography.

Common Interests

  • Irish, Welsh, and other Celtic mythological cycles
  • Gods and figures such as the Morrígan, Brigid, Lugh, the Dagda, Rhiannon, and others
  • Seasonal festivals (often aligned with the modern Wheel of the Year or older quarter days)
  • Connection to landscape, sacred sites, and ancestral heritage
  • Language learning and cultural respect as part of spiritual practice

Approaching the Material

Primary sources are limited and mediated through Christian-era manuscripts. Responsible practice usually involves studying scholarly translations and historical context rather than relying solely on modern popular summaries. Many practitioners also emphasize respect for living Celtic cultures and languages.
