Hellenism

Contemporary practice of ancient Greek religion — devotion to the Olympian gods and the broader Hellenic pantheon, often with a reconstructionist orientation.

Overview

Hellenism (or Hellenic polytheism) seeks to revive the religious practices of the ancient Greeks. Practitioners honor deities such as Zeus, Hera, Athena, Apollo, Artemis, Demeter, Dionysos, and many others. Emphasis is frequently placed on piety (eusebeia), reciprocal relationships with the gods through offerings and prayer, and careful study of classical sources.

Practice Themes

  • Daily and festival offerings (libations, incense, food)
  • Study of mythology, philosophy, and historical religion
  • Household worship and larger communal festivals
  • Respect for classical virtues and ethical frameworks
  • Interest in both popular religion and philosophical approaches (e.g., Neoplatonism)

Reconstruction and Adaptation

Many Hellenists lean toward reconstructionism — using primary texts, archaeology, and scholarly research to inform practice as accurately as possible. Others adapt more freely while remaining centered on the Greek gods. Both approaches exist within the broader community.
