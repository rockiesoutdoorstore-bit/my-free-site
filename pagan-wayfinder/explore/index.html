Explore Paths

Modern paganism contains many distinct (and sometimes overlapping) traditions. These overviews are starting points, not exhaustive definitions.

Wicca

A modern initiatory and non-initiatory tradition emphasizing ritual, the Wheel of the Year, and often dualistic deity.

Learn about Wicca →

Green Witchcraft

Nature-centered practice focused on plants, the land, herbalism, and living in rhythm with the seasons.

Learn about Green Witchcraft →

Druidry

Contemporary paths inspired by the ancient druids — often emphasizing nature, poetry, philosophy, and seasonal rites.

Learn about Druidry →

Heathenry

Modern practice rooted in the pre-Christian religions of Northern Europe, especially Germanic and Norse cultures.

Learn about Heathenry →

Hellenism

Revival and reconstruction of ancient Greek religion, focusing on the Olympian gods and classical piety.

Learn about Hellenism →

Celtic Paganism

Paths drawing on the mythologies, languages, and cultural traditions of the Celtic-speaking peoples.

Learn about Celtic Paganism →

Norse Paganism

Focus on the gods, myths, and worldview of the Norse and wider Germanic world (overlaps with Heathenry).

Learn about Norse Paganism →

Eclectic Paganism

Personal synthesis drawing from multiple sources rather than a single historical or cultural tradition.

Learn about Eclectic Paganism →

Reconstructionism

Approaches that prioritize historical and archaeological sources to rebuild ancient religious practices as accurately as possible.

Learn about Reconstructionism →

A note on labels

Many people practice across more than one of these categories, or prefer not to use labels at all. These pages exist to help you orient yourself — not to put you in a box.

If you’re unsure where to start, try the Path Quiz or the Beginner’s Guide.
