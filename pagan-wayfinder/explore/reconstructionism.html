Reconstructionism

An approach to modern pagan practice that prioritizes historical, archaeological, and textual evidence in order to rebuild ancient religious systems as accurately as possible.

Core Principle

Reconstructionist pagans treat ancient sources — literary, epigraphic, archaeological, and comparative — as the primary guide for belief and practice. The goal is not to invent a new religion with ancient flavor, but to recover and live within the religious worldview of a specific historical culture as faithfully as the evidence allows.

Common Reconstructionist Paths

  • Hellenic reconstructionism
  • Roman / Religio Romana reconstructionism
  • Heathen / Germanic reconstructionism
  • Celtic reconstructionism (Irish, Gaulish, etc.)
  • Kemetic (ancient Egyptian) reconstructionism
  • And others focused on specific cultures

Method and Limitations

Reconstruction requires ongoing study and a willingness to say “we don’t know” when the evidence is silent. Modern practitioners must still make reasoned choices about how to adapt ancient practices to contemporary life. Most reconstructionists accept that perfect restoration is impossible, but they hold historical grounding as a central value.
